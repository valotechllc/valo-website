Sample Cybersecurity Risk Assessment

A proof-of-work example showing how VALO translates technical and compliance findings into business risk, ownership, and an actionable remediation roadmap.

Fictional demonstration

This is not a client case study. The organization, findings, systems, and risk values below are fictional and are provided only to demonstrate the structure and decision-quality of a typical VALO deliverable.

Executive context

Example organization: Northstar Specialty Clinic, a fictional 85-employee healthcare practice using Microsoft 365, cloud-hosted clinical applications, a managed network, and several third-party business associates.

Assessment objective: Identify material cybersecurity risks, evaluate selected HIPAA Security Rule safeguards and NIST CSF 2.0 practices, validate key technical controls, and produce a prioritized 90-day remediation plan.

12material findings documented
5high-priority risks requiring leadership ownership
90 daysinitial remediation horizon

Executive summary

The example environment has a functional IT foundation but lacks consistent security governance around identity, privileged access, vendor oversight, incident readiness, and remediation accountability. The most urgent exposure is not a single missing tool. It is the combination of incomplete MFA enforcement, excessive administrative privilege, weak evidence of third-party oversight, and the absence of a formal risk-remediation cadence.

The recommended first phase focuses on reducing identity compromise risk, establishing accountable control ownership, documenting the highest-risk vendors, and creating a repeatable process for tracking remediation. Longer-term work should strengthen endpoint governance, logging, incident exercises, and evidence retention.

90-day remediation roadmap

The purpose of the roadmap is to convert findings into decisions and owned work—not to generate an endless list of controls.

0–30 days

  • Enforce MFA and block legacy authentication.
  • Inventory privileged accounts and remove unnecessary roles.
  • Assign executive owners to all high risks.
  • Create the central risk and remediation register.

31–60 days

  • Tier critical vendors and collect security evidence.
  • Review Conditional Access and administrative controls.
  • Formalize incident response roles and contact paths.
  • Document exceptions and risk acceptance decisions.

61–90 days

  • Conduct an incident-response tabletop exercise.
  • Review endpoint and logging coverage.
  • Validate closure evidence for remediated findings.
  • Present risk status and remaining decisions to leadership.

What a real engagement can include

Depending on scope, a VALO assessment can include an executive summary, risk register, prioritized findings, framework mapping, evidence review, control-owner matrix, remediation roadmap, POA&M-style tracking, architecture observations, and leadership briefing. Technical validation is tailored to the environment rather than treated as a generic checklist.
