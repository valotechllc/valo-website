Virtual CISO & Security Governance

Fractional security leadership that gives risk, controls, architecture and compliance an accountable owner.

A vCISO is a governance function, not an advisory hotline

Technology teams can operate systems without owning enterprise cybersecurity risk. A vCISO closes that accountability gap by establishing how security decisions are made, which risks require treatment or acceptance, who owns each control, what evidence proves the control is operating, and what leadership needs to see.

VALO's vCISO model combines governance, risk and compliance with security architecture experience. The result is a program that can move from executive expectations and framework requirements into policies, control patterns, remediation plans and measurable operating practices.

Core responsibilities

Security leadership with defined accountability.

Cybersecurity Strategy

Security strategy, program charter, target-state roadmap, priorities, investment decisions and alignment between business risk and technical work.

Governance & Policy

Policy and standards lifecycle, control ownership, RACI, exception management, risk acceptance, governance calendar and security steering cadence.

Enterprise Risk

Risk register ownership, inherent and residual risk analysis, treatment plans, risk appetite discussions, KRIs, third-party risk and remediation aging.

Executive & Board Reporting

Security scorecards, top risks, KPI/KRI trends, audit status, remediation progress, decision items and clear translation of technical exposure into business impact.

Engagement models

VALO scopes vCISO engagements around the actual governance burden rather than forcing every client into the same package. Organizations building a program from the ground up may need a foundational governance cadence. Organizations facing audits, regulated growth, customer assurance pressure or multiple frameworks may need deeper compliance ownership, evidence operations and executive reporting.

Foundational Governance

For organizations that need accountable security leadership, an enterprise risk register, policy governance, a prioritized roadmap and recurring executive reporting.

Compliance-Driven Governance

For organizations under HIPAA, SOC 2, GLBA, CMMC or similar pressure. Adds deeper control mapping, evidence operations, audit readiness, third-party risk and GRC workflow support.
